# draw-ascii · dataviz canon

The greats worth studying, and where to find them. When you want to draw a concept well,
steal from people who already did — the lesson usually survives translation to text (Minard's
melting army becomes a funnel; Beck's topology becomes a clean box-and-line diagram).
Companion to [gallery.md](gallery.md) and [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Living list — append a line
under the right heading as you find more.

## Marvels — visualizations worth studying

Each one teaches a move a *text* diagram can use. (Titles are searchable; images are one lookup away.)

- **A Chart of Biography** — Joseph Priestley, 1765 — the first timeline; lifespans as bars on a time axis. *Time is an axis.*
- **The Commercial & Political Atlas** — William Playfair, 1786 — invented the line & bar chart (and the pie, 1801). *Most chart forms trace back here.*
- **Broad Street cholera map** — John Snow, 1854 — plotted deaths on a street map and found the pump. *Put data where it happened; position is an axis.*
- **Diagram of the Causes of Mortality ("coxcomb")** — Florence Nightingale, 1858 — the shape itself argued, and moved a government. *Form can carry the point, not just the number.*
- **Napoleon's March** — Charles Minard, 1869 — six variables in one flow; Tufte's "best statistical graphic ever drawn." *One layout can hold several dimensions.*
- **The Periodic Table** — Dmitri Mendeleev, 1869 — a grid whose *gaps* predicted undiscovered elements. *A good grid reveals what's missing.*
- **Data portraits of Black America** — W.E.B. Du Bois, 1900 (Paris Exposition) — modernist hand-drawn infographics as argument. *Design is rhetoric.*
- **Hertzsprung–Russell diagram** — Ejnar Hertzsprung & Henry Norris Russell, ~1911 — one scatterplot organizes every star. *The right two axes classify a whole field.*
- **London Underground map** — Harry Beck, 1933 — threw away geography for topology. *Draw the mental model, not the literal thing* — why sequence and state diagrams work.
- **Gapminder / 200 Countries, 200 Years** — Hans Rosling, 2006–2010 — animated bubbles, near-zero chartjunk. *Strip to one moving idea.*
- **Warming Stripes** — Ed Hawkins, 2018 — axis-free climate stripes that went global. *Remove everything and the trend still reads.*
- **xkcd "Money" / "Radiation"** — Randall Munroe — nested scales made intuitive. *Zoom levels beat a single impossible axis.*

## The shelf — books

- **Edward Tufte** — *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983), then *Envisioning Information* (1990), *Visual Explanations* (1997), *Beautiful Evidence* (2006) — the foundation: data-ink, chartjunk, small multiples.
- **Alberto Cairo** — *The Functional Art* (2012), *The Truthful Art* (2016), *How Charts Lie* (2019) — visualization as a functional, honest tool; the last is the best on reading charts critically.
- **Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic** — *Storytelling with Data* (2015) — the practical decluttering-and-narrative handbook.
- **Stephen Few** — *Show Me the Numbers* (2004) — tables and graphs done right.
- **Nathan Yau** — *Visualize This* (2011) — hands-on, from the FlowingData blog.
- **Hans Rosling** — *Factfulness* (2018) — data as a correction to your worldview.
- **Scott Berinato** — *Good Charts* (2016, HBR) — dataviz for business persuasion.

## Living sources — blogs & sites

- [Information is Beautiful](https://informationisbeautiful.net) — David McCandless — bold, minimalist infographics.
- [The Pudding](https://pudding.cool) — interactive visual essays; the modern state of the art.
- [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org) — Max Roser — rigorous global metrics, cleanly charted.
- [Junk Charts](https://junkcharts.com) — Kaiser Fung — before/after critique; learn by watching bad charts get fixed.
- [Datawrapper blog](https://blog.datawrapper.de) — Lisa Charlotte Muth — the most practical writing on color and craft.
- [Nightingale](https://nightingaledvs.com) — the Data Visualization Society's journal — history, method, book reviews.
- [datavizblog.com](https://datavizblog.com) — history deep-dives (the Minard flow-map series is here).
- [Gapminder](https://gapminder.org) — Rosling's tools and data, free to explore.
